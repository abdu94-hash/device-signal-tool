About the book

This book shows how medical device manufacturers turn complaints, returned devices, registry data, clinical follow-up and device-generated data into safety decisions that are timely, based on evidence, and able to withstand audit. It follows the whole signal lifecycle inside the quality system: detection, validation, prioritization, root cause, integrated assessment, risk management file update, field action, communication and closure.

It is written for vigilance and post-market surveillance specialists, complaint-handling leads, safety physicians, risk management engineers, quality and CAPA owners, and regulatory affairs staff. Eight fictional device cases run through the chapters. The book is a companion to Signal Management in Pharmacovigilance by the same author.

The web tool

Six modules reproduce the book's worked analyses. Each has a "Load book example" button, and every field starts empty.

  • Trend screen: p-chart limits, the 2-of-3 run rule, a standardized CUSUM and an exact binomial check (Chapter 8)
  • Disproportionality and registry O/E: PRR and ROR with 95% intervals, and O/E with exact Poisson intervals and funnel limits (Chapter 9)
  • Time to failure: a life table with Kaplan–Meier survival and Greenwood intervals (Chapter 10)
  • Prioritization: the five-domain scale with bands, the floor rule, urgency triggers and recorded overrides (Chapter 13)
  • Risk-file update: the illustrative 5×5 matrix, with the region before and after the signal (Chapter 18)
  • Decision record: a printable record that keeps the conclusion separate from the status (Chapters 19 and 23)

Open the web tool Open the Case Lab

Educational use. Every tool is an example to adapt to your own quality system. Scores, bands, matrices and thresholds are illustrative company designs, not regulatory requirements. The web tool is not validated software, and a record it produces is not a controlled quality record.

Using the files

Each file is a starting point. Before you use one in production, bring it under your document control, replace the example thresholds and scales with the values in your own PMS plan and risk management file, and validate any spreadsheet you rely on for decisions. All case names in the book and the files (Suturex, Glucalyn, Tessora, Kestran, Orvana, Veltrix, Lumora, Neoflo and Brevant) are fictional composites. Their numbers are illustrative.

Regulatory statements in the book reflect the law as of September 2026. Check current texts before relying on them.
